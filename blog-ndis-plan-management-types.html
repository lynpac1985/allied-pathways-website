Self-Managed, Plan-Managed or NDIA-Managed: Which NDIS Option Is Right for You?

AP Allied Pathways Team 26 September 2026 4 min read

One of the first decisions you'll make with a new NDIS plan is how it's managed — and it's a decision that affects which providers you can use and how much admin lands on your desk. Here's how the three options actually compare.

Self-managed

You pay providers directly and claim reimbursement through the NDIS myplace portal. This gives you the most flexibility — you can use any provider, registered or unregistered — but it also means the most paperwork, since you're responsible for keeping receipts and managing claims yourself.

Plan-managed

A Plan Manager pays your providers' invoices on your behalf, and you can still choose any registered or unregistered provider. Plan management is itself a funded support, so it doesn't cost you anything extra out of your other budgets. This is a popular middle-ground option — flexibility on providers, without the admin of self-management.

NDIA-managed (Agency-managed)

The NDIA pays providers directly, which means the least amount of admin for you — but you can only use providers registered with the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission.

Can I mix and match?

Yes. It's common to have different budget categories managed in different ways — for example, your Capacity Building supports plan-managed, while Core Supports are self-managed. Your Support Coordinator or Plan Manager can help set this up correctly.

Which one is "best"?

There isn't a single right answer — it depends on how much admin you want to take on and how much flexibility you want in choosing providers. Allied Pathways works with self-managed, plan-managed and NDIA-managed participants, so your management type won't limit your access to our team.
